\section*{Problem 1.5}
Simplify Eq. (1.20) by assuming that there is only one sample $S_{1j}$ whose composition is measured. Consider the case where the composition of the $p$ factors is known, but their proportions in the sample are unknown, and rewrite Eq. (10.2) in the form $ \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{Gm}$.
\subsection*{Solution}
Looking at the matrix form of Eq. (1.20) $\mathbf{S = CF}$
\begin{equation*}
\begin{bmatrix}
S_{11} & & & & \\
& \ddots & & & \\
& & S_{ij} & & \\
& & & \ddots & \\
& & & & S_{NM}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
C_{11} & & & & \\
& \ddots & & & \\
& & C_{ik} & & \\
& & & \ddots & \\
& & & & C_{Np}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
F_{11} & & & & \\
& \ddots & & & \\
& & F_{kj} & & \\
& & & \ddots & \\
& & & & F_{pM}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

If we have $N=1$ then this turns into 

\begin{equation*}
\begin{bmatrix}
S_{11} & \cdots & S_{1M}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
C_{11} & \cdots & C_{1k} & \cdots & C_{1p}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
F_{11} & & & & \\
& \ddots & & & \\
& & F_{kj} & & \\
& & & \ddots & \\
& & & & F_{pM}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

Using the identity relating taking the transpose of a matrix and matrix multiplication; $(\mathbf{AB})^T = \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{A}^T$, we get


\begin{equation*}
\begin{bmatrix}
S_{11}\\
\vdots \\
S_{j1} \\
\vdots \\
S_{M1}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
F_{11} & & & & \\
& \ddots & & & \\
& & F_{jk} & & \\
& & & \ddots & \\
& & & & F_{Mp}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
C_{11}\\
\vdots \\
C_{k1} \\
\vdots \\
C_{p1}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

This gives us the form  we are looking for with $\mathbf{d = S}$, $\mathbf{G = F}$, $\mathbf{m = C}$
